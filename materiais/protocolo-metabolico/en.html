Chapter 01

The stuck metabolism

For years you tried everything, and your body didn’t respond. There’s a reason — and it isn’t your willpower. First, let’s get to know the enemy.

Metabolism is the rate at which your body burns energy. From breathing and your heartbeat to repairing cells — everything needs energy. The energy you burn doing nothing, just to stay alive, is called your basal metabolic rate — and it accounts for most of the calories you burn in a day.

Now the bad news: this rate isn’t fixed. It drops with age, with hormonal changes and — most importantly — when you lose muscle. Muscle is your body’s furnace; even at rest it burns many times more energy than fat does. The more muscle you have, the bigger your furnace.

Why is the handbrake on?

If you do nothing about it, after the age of 30 you lose 3 to 8% of your muscle every decade. This is called sarcopenia. The furnace shrinks, but your appetite and habits stay the same. The result: you start gaining weight even though you eat the same as always. It’s not your fault — the shrinking furnace is to blame.

Add strict diets to that. Your body reads a sudden cut in calories as a threat of starvation. What does it do to survive? It starts saving fuel, slows your metabolism down and uses muscle as a source of energy. This is called adaptive thermogenesis. Every crash diet pulls the handbrake on again and locks the engine tighter. That’s the science behind why losing weight gets harder with every diet.

60–70%of your daily calories are burned at rest alone
3–8per cent of muscle lost every decade after 30
20–30%of the energy in protein is used just to digest it

After 40, the equation changes

As menopause approaches, oestrogen levels fall. Oestrogen isn’t just a reproductive hormone — it also affects where your body stores fat. When it drops, fat moves from your hips to your tummy — the place you like least in the mirror. At the same time, insulin sensitivity drops: your body finds it harder to handle sugar and more easily turns the excess into fat.

Then there’s stress. Long-term stress raises the hormone cortisol. High cortisol encourages fat to settle around your tummy and makes your body hold on to more water — so part of what you see in the mirror in the morning as puffiness isn’t even fat, it’s retained water. The good news: with the right approach, this puffiness is the first thing to go.

What does the science say?
Most of the weight you’ll lose in the first week isn’t fat, but water and glycogen (the sugar your muscles and liver store). That’s completely normal and nothing to worry about — it’s a sign your body is starting to let go of puffiness. Real fat loss comes with patience, week after week. We aim for both: quick, visible relief and lasting change.

Why will it be different this time?

Because this time we’re not fighting against your metabolism — we’re working with it. Instead of starving your body and setting off its starvation alarm, we feed it and gently support thermogenesis — burning calories by producing heat. We’ll use three keys:

  • Protein and movement — protect and grow the furnace (your muscles).
  • Thermogenic plants — gently turn up the energy you use each day.
  • Water, sleep and daily rhythm — clear puffiness and bring your hormones back into balance.

The Challenge has already turned the first key. This book adds the other two. Together they make a full set of keys that opens the locked door.

Looking at the results of the women we coach, we noticed a pattern: even though they did exactly the same Challenge, some women firmed up much faster than others. The only difference? The specific ingredients they added to the tea they drank during the day.

Let’s be direct: drinking tea for pleasure is one thing, and drinking tea to steer your body towards burning fat is something completely different. Ordinary tea with a sugar cube does almost nothing for your metabolism. Even plain green tea only works on the surface. The secret was never the tea itself — it was about giving the right ingredients, at the right time, to a body that’s already been switched on.

What is the thermogenic effect?

Some plants — green tea, ginger, chilli, cinnamon — cause a slight rise in heat in your body. Producing that heat takes energy, and that energy comes from calories. So these plants can raise the calories you burn at rest by a small but meaningful percentage. One cup won’t work miracles; but when you drink it for 28 days, every day, at the right time, those small increases add up. Here’s the protocol’s formula: small effect × consistency × right timing = visible result.

What does the science say?
Green tea catechins (its natural plant compounds, especially EGCG), taken together with caffeine, can measurably increase the energy you use over 24 hours and the amount of fat you burn (Dulloo et al., 1999; meta-analysis by Hursel et al., 2009). The effect is moderate — it’s not magic, but used correctly, it’s real support.

Golden brewing rules

Brewing it right draws out all of the tea’s power. Brewing it wrong makes it bitter and breaks down the valuable compounds. Three numbers are all you need:

80°Cideal water temperature for green tea — not boiling
2–3 minbrewing time for green tea; longer = bitter
90°Cfor herbs and roots; brew for 5–7 min

  • Boil the water, take the lid off and wait 3–4 minutes — that gives you the right temperature for green tea (about 80°C).
  • Never pour boiling water over green tea; the catechins break down and it turns bitter.
  • After brewing, take out the leaves or tea bag; the longer they sit, the harsher the tea.
  • Squeeze in the lemon once the tea has cooled a little — very hot tea destroys some of the vitamin C.

The water protocol

Tea matters, but the real medicine is water. Without enough water you won’t get rid of puffiness, and your metabolism won’t work at full strength. It sounds like a paradox, but it’s true: when your body is short of water, it holds on to more. When you drink plenty, your body lets go of the water it was holding.

  • Start your day with a glass of lukewarm water with a few drops of lemon — even before your tea.
  • Goal for the whole day: 2–2.5 litres of water (teas don’t count).
  • A glass of water before every meal — it helps digestion and makes you feel full.
  • After 20:00, drink less so you don’t wake up at night.
The habit that holds the whole system together
Tea is an accelerator, not a shortcut. With every meal, eat a palm-sized portion of protein (eggs, yoghurt, chicken, beans or lentils, cheese) — it’s what protects your furnace (your muscles). Don’t starve yourself. This protocol is meant to speed you up while keeping you full.

Green tea and ginger

once a day
Ingredients

  • Cinnamon (ground)1 pinch

Method
  1. Boil the water, take the lid off, wait 3–4 min (about 80°C).

What does the science say?
Water retention depends largely on the balance of sodium and potassium. More potassium and less excess sodium make it easier for your body to let go of extra water. That’s why, when you “lose water”, your weight can drop by a few kg at once — that’s water — and it’s a good start.

Bonus ★ · A 90-second ritual

Four afternoon
tricks

That moment, usually in the afternoon, when you badly want a snack — from stress, boredom or worry, not hunger — can undo a whole day’s effort in a second. This 90-second ritual stops emotional hunger at the source.

First: real or emotional hunger?

Emotional hunger
  • It comes on suddenly: “now, right now”.
  • It wants something specific: chocolate, chips, pastry.
  • It comes from emotions, not your stomach.
  • Afterwards, you feel guilty.
Real hunger
  • It builds up slowly, gradually.
  • You’d eat almost anything.
  • Your stomach sends a signal (rumbling).
  • Afterwards, you feel full and calm.
What does the science say?
In the afternoon, cortisol and energy naturally dip; your body often mistakes this for “give me sugar”. And in a stressful moment, even one deep sigh (the so-called physiological sigh) can quickly calm your nervous system and weaken a sudden craving. You don’t need food — just a 90-second break.

The 90-second ritual

When a craving hits, before you reach for food, do these steps in order. A minute and a half in total. Usually the craving passes before you finish.

  1. Stop and name it (10 s)

    Move your hand away from the food. Tell yourself: “This isn’t hunger, it’s an emotion”. Just noticing it switches off your brain’s autopilot.

  2. Take a double breath (30 s)

    Breathe in through your nose, then take one more small breath on top (to fill your lungs), then breathe out slowly through your mouth. Repeat 4–5 times. This “physiological sigh” lowers stress right away.

RiseMe · Four afternoon tricksBonus
  1. Drink a glass of water or tea (30 s)

    Sip slowly. Thirst often disguises itself as hunger; a warm tea calms both your stomach and your mind. If you’re in the cleanse phase, it may be just the right time for your tea.

  2. Change your surroundings (20 s)

    Stand up, leave the room, go to the window, relax your shoulders. A change of surroundings breaks the chain that set off the craving. By the time you come back, the urge is usually gone.

Still hungry?
If the urge doesn’t pass after the ritual, it may be real hunger — and that’s fine. Then eat something with protein, mindfully: a handful of nuts, a yoghurt, a hard-boiled egg. The point isn’t to starve yourself, but to tell emotional eating apart from real hunger.

You know that afternoon tiredness that makes it hard to make it to the evening. This protocol takes just 3 minutes and lifts your energy straight away — as if you’d just woken up from a refreshing midday nap. And best of all: it also deepens your sleep at night.

  1. Energy breathing · 60 seconds

    Sit up straight or stand. Breathe quickly and rhythmically through your nose (in and out about once a second), 20 times; then finish with one deep breath. It gives your brain fresh oxygen and clears your mind. If you feel dizzy, slow down or stop.

  2. A burst of movement · 90 seconds

    30 s of marching or jogging in place, 30 s of shoulder and arm circles, 30 s of stretching. A slightly faster heart rate steadies your blood sugar and shakes off that heavy drowsiness that makes you dream of nothing but a nap.

  3. Step into the light · 30 seconds

Why does it improve your sleep at night?
Light and movement during the day sharpen your body’s day–night rhythm (circadian rhythm). The more clearly you are “awake” in the day, the more clearly you “sleep” at night. And good sleep — as you’ll see in a moment — is a hidden fat-burning engine.

Burning fat while you sleep

For your metabolism to work around the clock, it’s not only what you do in the day that counts, but also what you do at night. Sleep is the most underrated — and at the same time the most powerful — weapon in this protocol. Here’s the science of keeping the furnace going while you sleep.

Lack of sleep directly throws your appetite out of balance. When you sleep too little, your fullness hormone — leptin — drops, and your hunger hormone — ghrelin — rises. So the next day you wake up hungrier and craving more sweet things. At the same time, your stress hormone, cortisol, rises, which encourages fat to settle around your tummy.

It also works the other way round: in deep, long enough sleep, your body releases growth hormone. This hormone rebuilds muscle and helps your body use fat. A real body transformation starts in your workouts, but finishes in your sleep. Without good sleep, you won’t break through a plateau.

Slow-digesting protein (casein is the main protein in milk) feeds your muscles with amino acids all night long; it supports recovery and fullness and reduces morning hunger. A small bowl before bed is enough.

Magnesium helps relax your muscles and calm your nervous system; it’s the classic mineral for supporting sleep quality.

7 secrets

Two women drink the same teas. Why does one see results faster? The difference lies in small details. None of them is hard — but together they multiply your result.

  1. Never pour boiling water over green tea

    The ideal temperature is 80°C. Boiling water (100°C) destroys the valuable catechins and makes the tea bitter. Boil the water, take the lid off, wait 3–4 minutes and only then pour. This one change alone clearly boosts the power of your tea.

  2. Always add lemon

    A little lemon in green tea helps the catechins stay stable in your digestive system and be absorbed better. It also freshens the taste. Squeeze it in once the tea has cooled a little, to keep the vitamin C.

  3. Don’t drink strong tea with iron-rich meals

    The tannins in tea (natural compounds that give it its dry taste) can reduce how much plant iron you absorb. Don’t have tea with your main iron-rich meals — with lentils, beans, spinach; drink it between meals instead. A small change of timing, a big difference.

  4. Start your day with protein

    Protein takes more energy to digest than any other part of your food (the thermic effect of food). Eggs, yoghurt or cheese for breakfast light the first metabolic spark of the day and keep you full until lunchtime.

  5. Cut sugar out of your tea completely

    Even one sugar cube can quietly cancel out the tea’s thermogenic effect and — by raising your insulin — slow down fat burning. If you must sweeten it, use cinnamon, a drop of lemon or a little honey — but not sugar.

  6. Count your steps — a hidden part of the furnace

    For most women, everyday movement outside workouts (NEAT) burns more calories than even the exercises in the plan. Aim for 7,000–8,000 steps a day: short walks, stairs, standing tasks. While the tea works from the inside, your steps work from the outside.

  7. Go for regularity, not intensity

    The most powerful factor in changing your body is consistency. A perfect routine done now and then will never beat a simple one done every day. For 28 days, don’t skip the small steps — that’s the whole secret.

Frequently asked questions

Everything that might come to mind before you start. If your answer isn’t here, once you’ve unlocked the content, write to us through the app.

Will the teas keep me awake?

Green tea contains caffeine, which is why it’s only in the morning teas (08:00 and 11:00) — don’t drink it in the afternoon. The hibiscus tea and the evening teas — fennel with cinnamon, and chamomile — contain no caffeine and won’t disturb your sleep. If you’re sensitive to caffeine, reduce the amount of green tea.

I’m pregnant or breastfeeding. Can I follow the protocol?

Some herbs aren’t recommended during this time. Before starting the protocol, be sure to talk to your doctor, and don’t follow it without their approval. Your health comes first.

I take medication regularly — what then?

Some herbs can interact with medicines (especially blood thinners, blood pressure medicines or diabetes medicines). Please talk to your doctor before you start drinking the teas.

How much weight will I lose?

It depends on the person. Most of the weight that disappears in the first week is water and puffiness — it’s quick and motivating, but it isn’t fat. Real fat loss builds week after week. Don’t let the scales be your only measure; look at the mirror, your clothes and your energy.

If I miss a day, do I have to start again?

No. One missed day doesn’t ruin anything. The next day, you simply get back to the plan. What counts isn’t one perfect day, but regularity over time. Don’t blame yourself, just keep going.

I’m going through menopause. Will this system work for me?

Yes. During menopause your metabolism slows down, but it doesn’t stop. For you, protein, strength training and good sleep become even more important — this book highlights all three. Be patient; at any age, your body responds to the right signals.

Can I drink all the teas at once?

No — each tea has its own time, and how it works is tied to that time. Stick to the calendar: the ignition tea in the morning, that phase’s tea at midday or in the afternoon, the stabilising tea in the evening. Timing is half the success of this system.
